A conference schedule is a promise to a few hundred people at once. The talks that wreck it rarely mean to. They just cannot see the time. Give every session a visible clock.

## One Mac, every session

Set up a Mac where the speaker can see it and start a countdown for each slot: "25 min" for the talk, "5" for questions, "10" for the break. Take the timer [full screen](/blog/full-screen-countdown-timer-mac) so it reads from the lectern, or keep it large in a corner of the room display.

## Lectures on the clock

For a lecture, the timer works facing either direction. Facing you, it paces the material. Facing the class, it time-boxes discussions and exercises: type "5 min" for group work and let the countdown call time.

## Session changeover in one line

Next speaker, same slot: reset and start. Different slot: type the new length and press Return. The timer is ready before the next presenter has plugged in. A [speaker timer](/blog/speaker-timer) setup for the whole day takes less effort than announcing time warnings ever did.

## Breaks that end on time

Breaks drift worse than talks. Put a [full screen countdown](/blog/full-screen-countdown-timer-mac) on the room display during the break. People pace their coffee against it, and the next session starts with the room already seated.

The schedule is the product. A visible clock is the cheapest way to keep it.
